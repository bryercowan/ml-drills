    Key Papers¶

    ~15 papers every ML engineer should read. For each: summary, what to focus on, and estimated read time.

    Read the abstract and introduction first. Then the method section. Skip proofs on first pass — focus on the what and why, not the proof that it works.

    Foundational Architecture¶

    Attention Is All You Need (Vaswani et al., 2017)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/1706.03762

    Summary: Introduces the Transformer architecture, replacing recurrence entirely with self-attention. Shows that attention alone, combined with positional encoding and feedforward layers, achieves state-of-the-art on machine translation.

    Focus on: The scaled dot-product attention formula, multi-head attention, positional encoding, and the encoder-decoder structure. Figure 1 and Section 3 are the core.

    Read time: 2-3 hours

    Flash Attention (Dao et al., 2022)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2205.14135

    Summary: Makes attention O(N) in memory (instead of O(N²)) by restructuring the computation to be IO-aware — using tiling and recomputation to avoid materializing the full N×N attention matrix in GPU HBM. Achieves 2-4× wall-clock speedup.

    Focus on: The IO-awareness argument (Section 2), why the naive implementation is memory-bound, and the tiling algorithm. The key insight is that compute is cheap but memory bandwidth is the bottleneck.

    Read time: 2-3 hours (math-heavy; focus on Sections 1-3)

    Fine-Tuning & Efficiency¶

    LoRA: Low-Rank Adaptation of Large Language Models (Hu et al., 2021)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2106.09685

    Summary: Proposes freezing pretrained weights and adding trainable low-rank decomposition matrices (A and B) to attention layers. Reduces trainable parameters by 10,000× while matching full fine-tuning quality on GPT-3.

    Focus on: The low-rank hypothesis (Section 2), the method (Section 4), and the empirical rank analysis showing that weight updates during fine-tuning are intrinsically low-rank.

    Read time: 1.5-2 hours

    QLoRA (Dettmers et al., 2023)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2305.14314

    Summary: Combines 4-bit quantization of the frozen base model with LoRA adapters, enabling fine-tuning of a 65B parameter model on a single 48GB GPU. Introduces NormalFloat4 data type and double quantization to minimize memory without losing quality.

    Focus on: The quantization scheme (NF4), paged optimizers for memory spikes, and the finding that 4-bit LoRA matches 16-bit full fine-tuning. Section 3 is the core method.

    Read time: 2 hours

    Generative Models¶

    Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models / DDPM (Ho et al., 2020)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239

    Summary: Establishes the modern diffusion framework: define a forward noising process, train a network to reverse it, generate by iterative denoising from pure noise. Shows the simplified training objective (predict the noise) works better than the variational bound.

    Focus on: The forward process definition, the simplified loss function (Equation 14), and the connection between noise prediction and score matching. Section 3 is everything.

    Read time: 3-4 hours (math-heavy but worth it)

    Diffusion Policy (Chi et al., 2023)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137

    Summary: Applies diffusion models to robot action prediction, treating the action sequence as the "image" to denoise. Shows that diffusion's ability to model multimodal distributions makes it superior to deterministic policies for manipulation tasks where multiple valid actions exist.

    Focus on: Why multimodal action distributions matter for robotics (Section 1), the architecture (conditioning on observations), and the comparison with behavior cloning baselines.

    Read time: 2 hours

    Vision¶

    CLIP: Learning Transferable Visual Models from Natural Language Supervision (Radford et al., 2021)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2103.00020

    Summary: Trains dual image/text encoders with contrastive learning on 400M image-text pairs from the internet. The resulting model can classify images zero-shot by comparing image embeddings to text embeddings of class descriptions, matching supervised models without any task-specific training.

    Focus on: The contrastive pre-training objective (Figure 1), zero-shot transfer via text prompts, and the scaling analysis. The simplicity of the method relative to its power is the key insight.

    Read time: 2-3 hours

    ConvNeXt: A ConvNet for the 2020s (Liu et al., 2022)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2201.03545

    Summary: Systematically modernizes a standard ResNet using design choices from Transformers (larger kernels, fewer activation functions, layer norm instead of batch norm, GELU) and matches ViT performance without using attention. Proves that many of ViT's gains came from training recipes, not architecture.

    Focus on: The "modernization roadmap" (Section 2) — each change and its impact. This paper is uniquely educational because it's essentially an ablation study of what makes modern architectures work.

    Read time: 1.5-2 hours

    Conditioning¶

    FiLM: Visual Reasoning with a General Conditioning Layer (Perez et al., 2018)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/1709.07871

    Summary: Introduces Feature-wise Linear Modulation — conditioning a network by predicting per-channel scale (γ) and shift (β) parameters from auxiliary input. A simple, general-purpose conditioning mechanism that outperforms concatenation and addition for visual reasoning.

    Focus on: The FiLM layer definition (Section 3), how the conditioning network generates γ and β, and why feature-wise modulation is more expressive than simpler conditioning approaches.

    Read time: 1.5 hours

    Alignment & RL¶

    InstructGPT / Training Language Models to Follow Instructions with RLHF (Ouyang et al., 2022)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2203.02155

    Summary: The paper that launched the RLHF era. Trains GPT-3 to follow instructions using: (1) supervised fine-tuning on demonstrations, (2) training a reward model on human preferences, (3) PPO optimization against the reward model. A 1.3B InstructGPT is preferred to the 175B base GPT-3.

    Focus on: The three-phase training pipeline (Section 3), reward model training, the KL penalty to prevent reward hacking, and the human evaluation methodology.

    Read time: 2-3 hours

    DPO: Direct Preference Optimization (Rafailov et al., 2023)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2305.18290

    Summary: Shows that RLHF's reward modeling + PPO pipeline can be collapsed into a single supervised loss. Derives a closed-form mapping between reward functions and optimal policies, eliminating the need for a separate reward model and RL training loop.

    Focus on: The derivation connecting reward models to the optimal policy (Section 3), the DPO loss function, and why it's equivalent to RLHF under the Bradley-Terry preference model. Elegantly simple.

    Read time: 2 hours

    DeepSeek-R1 / GRPO¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2501.12948

    Summary: Introduces Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which eliminates the value function/critic from policy optimization. Samples multiple outputs per prompt, scores them, and uses within-group relative rankings as advantages. Shows that pure RL (without SFT) can develop chain-of-thought reasoning as emergent behavior.

    Focus on: The GRPO algorithm (how group-relative advantages replace the critic), the emergence of reasoning without explicit chain-of-thought training, and the training pipeline.

    Read time: 3-4 hours (long paper; focus on Sections 2-3)

    Scaling & Robotics¶

    Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models (Kaplan et al., 2020)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361

    Summary: Establishes power-law relationships between model performance and compute, dataset size, and parameter count. Shows that performance improves predictably with scale, and that larger models are more sample-efficient. The foundation for all scaling decisions at frontier labs.

    Focus on: The three scaling laws (Figures 1-3), the finding that model size matters more than training time for fixed compute, and the implications for optimal resource allocation.

    Read time: 2 hours

    Chinchilla / Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models (Hoffmann et al., 2022)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2203.15556

    Summary: Overturns the Kaplan scaling laws by showing that model size and training data should be scaled equally. Demonstrates that most large LLMs were significantly undertrained — a 70B model trained on 4× more data matches a 280B model. Changed how the field allocates training compute.

    Focus on: The three approaches to estimating compute-optimal allocation (Section 3), and how Chinchilla (70B, 1.4T tokens) outperforms Gopher (280B, 300B tokens) with 4× less compute at inference.

    Read time: 2 hours

    RT-2: Vision-Language-Action Models (Brohan et al., 2023)¶

    Link: arxiv.org/abs/2307.15818

    Summary: Turns a vision-language model (PaLM-E / PaLI-X) into a robot policy by representing actions as text tokens. Demonstrates that web-scale language and vision pretraining transfers to robotic manipulation, enabling generalization to novel objects and instructions without robotics-specific training.

    Focus on: How actions are tokenized and integrated into the VLM output space, the transfer learning setup (web pretraining → robot fine-tuning), and the generalization results showing emergent robotic capabilities.

    Read time: 2-3 hours
